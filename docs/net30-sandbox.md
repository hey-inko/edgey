# Net 30 sandbox sign-off

Keep `NET30_CHECKOUT_ENABLED=false` until finance signs off the lifecycle below. The storefront also requires an approved, unsuspended account and sufficient available credit before it creates a hold. Turning off the flag blocks new Net 30 attempts; it does not stop reconciliation of existing orders. This build uses Stripe test credentials only.

1. Configure a staging Stripe sandbox and the matching webhook signing secret. Listen for the card events already configured and for `invoice.finalized`, `invoice.sent`, `invoice.paid`, `invoice.payment_succeeded`, `invoice_payment.paid`, `invoice.voided`, `invoice.deleted`, `invoice.marked_uncollectible`, `invoice.overdue`, `credit_note.created`, `credit_note.voided`, `charge.refunded` and refund events. Stripe only emits some events when explicitly selected or configured; check invoice overdue settings separately. Confirm the Stripe reconciliation and email workers are scheduled and authorised.
2. Approve a staging trade application with £200 credit. Check that a retry uses the same Stripe customer and its verified billing details, and that the decision notice goes to the applicant once. Check that a rejected or suspended account can still use card checkout. The rejection reason is for staff, not the email.
3. With the staging flag on, order on account with a PO. In Stripe verify one manual invoice, a 30-day due date, the Edgey reference, the PO, the approved billing identity and the delivery address. Inspect the PDF, including the supplier and VAT details, and open the invoice from the buyer's account. Check the five-Z10 example: £99.75 goods, £6.95 delivery, £21.34 VAT and £128.04 due. Stripe sends an `invoice.sent` event in test mode but does not actually send its invoice email; verify the event and the PDF separately from the app's transactional emails.
4. Check that an issued invoice moves the gross amount from held to outstanding and lets warehouse pick and dispatch. Register a partial payment and settle the rest using Stripe's invoice payment allocations. Check the remaining balance, available credit, payment label, tracking and dispatch date after both steps. Replay a webhook after settlement and check that neither money nor stock moves again.
5. Cancel an unpaid, undispatched order in the warehouse queue. Confirm the allocation returns to available stock and the balance stays outstanding until Finance verifies the Stripe void. For a dispatched order, verify that a Stripe refund does not bring stock back: only a received and inspected return can do that. Check credit notes before and after payment, an overdue balance, and the separate uncollectible exception. Marking an invoice uncollectible does not clear Edgey's outstanding credit or suspend the account.
6. Exercise a lost response after invoice creation and after sending: retry the original checkout, or let reconciliation recover it. Compare the Stripe invoice ID, the order reference, the PO, and the credit ledger. In finance, review unreconciled or amount-mismatched cases; do not reissue the same order in the Dashboard. If the safe retry window passes and Stripe still cannot prove the invoice state, Edgey holds the credit and stock for review. Keep those holds until the original provider object is found and its state confirmed.

Do not detach payments, void credit notes, revise issued invoices or apply customer balance adjustments on Edgey invoices as part of ordinary operations. These changes cannot be silently translated to the existing ledger; finance must reconcile the provider history before enabling any such workflow. Confirm VAT registration and invoice legal details, the supported payment methods, initial limits, overdue ownership and the separate live-mode readiness checks before accepting real orders.
